## Overview

Human rhinovirus (HRV), the leading cause of the common cold, remains a persistent global pathogen despite widespread exposure and repeated immune priming. With more than 160 recognized types, HRV causes recurrent infections throughout life and has proven difficult to target through conventional vaccine strategies.

In this project, we investigated how HRV may evade host immune defenses through four interconnected layers:

1. **Genome composition** — region-specific CpG suppression and TpG enrichment
2. **Codon usage bias** — patterns potentially shaped by immune avoidance rather than translational efficiency
3. **Host gene expression** — transcriptional responses following HRV infection
4. **Sequence divergence** — evolutionary dynamics and sequence variability across HRV strains

We additionally compared these patterns with **West Nile Virus (WNV)**, a zoonotic flavivirus for which humans are a dead-end host.

Our findings reveal a set of complementary immune evasion mechanisms spanning viral genome composition, codon usage, host transcriptional responses, and viral evolution. Together, these mechanisms suggest that HRV's persistence may be driven not by a single immune evasion strategy, but by an interconnected system of adaptations that collectively reduce immune recognition.

---

## Research Questions

Our analysis investigated four major questions:

- How does HRV genome composition vary across genomic regions, particularly with respect to CpG and TpG frequencies?
- Does HRV exhibit codon usage patterns consistent with immune avoidance?
- How does HRV infection alter host gene expression and innate immune responses?
- How does sequence variability across HRV strains reflect evolutionary pressures associated with persistent human infection?

## Key Findings

Our analyses identified several interconnected patterns of immune evasion:

- **Region-specific CpG suppression and TpG enrichment** within the HRV genome
- **Codon usage bias** consistent with immune avoidance rather than solely translational optimization
- **Muted host innate immune activation** following HRV infection
- **High sequence divergence** shaped by sustained selective pressures in human hosts

Together, these findings support a multi-layered model in which distinct viral adaptations reinforce one another to reduce immune recognition and facilitate persistent circulation of HRV.

---

## Project Significance

Although individual mechanisms of HRV immune evasion have been studied previously, their interaction across genomic, transcriptional, and evolutionary scales has received less attention.

Our work proposes a **mechanism-based framework** for understanding HRV immune evasion, highlighting how multiple adaptations can function together as a cohesive strategy. This perspective may help inform future approaches to vaccine and antiviral development.
